Audience growth is broad. The next job is conversion.

The latest platform data confirms that Instagram remains the largest discovery engine and YouTube continues to split into two distinct jobs: Shorts create reach, while long-form creates depth. Since the July update, the production system has also expanded into full interview campaigns for Antonio Damasio and Sam Roberts, with Lisa's videos now queued and ready for editing.

Executive summary

Instagram recorded 902,355 views in the latest available 90-day account snapshot, with 99.2% coming from non-followers. YouTube recorded 74,392 views and 934.6 watch hours in its June 10–September 7 window.

The strongest strategic signal is not simply more reach. Only 12% of returning YouTube viewers watched both Shorts and videos, leaving a clear opportunity to improve short-form-to-long-form crossover and book-purchase intent. The next phase is to keep the interview pipeline moving while doubling down on the formats and hooks already producing discovery.

Next priorities

Double down on what is working, then build the next campaigns around it

Double down on discovery formats already working.
Keep Reels as Instagram's primary format and Shorts as YouTube's primary discovery engine. Gaming, culture, immediately understandable hooks, and concise practical or curiosity-led ideas are leading the current rankings, so future clip selection should favor those traits when the interview material supports them.

Improve Short → interview crossover.
Use the strongest Shorts as entry points to the exact long-form conversation they came from. The current 12% cross-format overlap is the clearest funnel weakness.

Raise the Shorts stay rate.
With 41.7% staying and 58.3% swiping away, opening hooks should be judged on immediate specificity, tension, and payoff. The first seconds remain the highest-leverage editing decision.

Make book conversion trackable.
Use dedicated links or campaign-specific landing pages for featured books so social activity can be connected to store traffic and sales instead of ending at platform engagement.

Edit Lisa's queued videos and prepare upcoming projects before production begins.
Move Lisa's videos through the established workflow next, while researching upcoming guests and books in advance. Build interview framing, clip angles, potential hooks, thumbnail directions, and sales positioning before each interview so production starts with a distribution plan rather than creating one afterward.

Protect focus.
Continue Instagram for discovery and YouTube for discovery plus depth. Add new platform-specific production only when there is a defined test, a measurable objective, and enough upside to justify the extra workload.

Methodology and limitations

This is not a 90-day client-report period. The report itself covers work completed since the July 31 update. However, the current performance source uses each platform's latest available analytics window. YouTube explicitly shows June 10–September 7, 2026 as the last 90 days. Instagram does not provide a custom date range matching August 1–September 8 in the supplied interface, so its current figures are treated as a cumulative snapshot rather than a direct period-over-period comparison.

July totals and current 90-day totals are therefore shown side by side only where useful for context. No percentage is labeled as month-over-month growth. Cross-platform views are not unique people and should not be added together.

Instagram account-level and content-level screenshots may reflect different reporting states. Content rankings are therefore presented as captured and are not mathematically reconciled to the account-level total.

YouTube's subscriber figures use different scopes. The channel overview reports a net change of -35 subscribers over the 90-day window, while the content view separately attributes +38 subscribers to Shorts and +70 to videos. The format-attributed figures are not added together or substituted for the channel-level net change.
